Nev’s Master Thesis

A working automation for a small service business, and a written argument about one decision inside it: which steps a computer can be trusted with, and which ones still need a person.

  • Built 25 workflows, 255 nodes
  • Across 5 kinds of business
  • Tested with 60 example messages
  • Runs on n8n + Claude
01

The idea, in one minute

Almost every small business runs the same five steps. Someone gets in touch, you arrange to look at the job, you send a price, you chase it up, and eventually you invoice.

The automation runs all five steps. It is not allowed to send a price on its own, though. It drafts one, and a person checks it before it goes out.

That rule is the argument. Anyone can build an automation and say so. The part worth a master’s is the reasoning underneath it: four of these steps run on their own, the fifth does not, and there is evidence for why the line sits there.

Let it decide

Much the same every time, and cheap to undo when it goes wrong. Sorting incoming messages. Suggesting appointment times. Sending a reminder.

Keep a person

Different every time, and expensive when it goes wrong. Pricing the work. Anything a customer cannot un-see once it has left your outbox.

02

Five kinds of business

The same five workflows have been built for five different trades. One skeleton underneath, with the vocabulary, the price list and the approval rules swapped out.

Only one of them is needed for the thesis itself. The other four exist so there is something to say about whether the idea travels between industries, and they turn out to disagree with each other in useful ways.

Electrical contractor

Someone visits, looks at the work, then prices it.

The most straightforward of the five, and the one everything else is measured against.

Dental practice

A consultation, then a treatment plan with prices.

The risky part moves to the front. Sorting symptoms from a message is nearly a medical decision. Once the diagnosis is fixed, though, the pricing is almost mechanical. The opposite shape to the contractor.

Physiotherapy practice

An assessment, then a course of treatment. An insurer often pays.

The strongest argument in the set for keeping a person involved. Some messages describe symptoms that need a human straight away. One of them is written so calmly that a careless system would just book it for next Tuesday.

Tax & accounting firm

A scoping call, then an engagement letter.

There is no visit at all. The work gets described, never inspected. A conflict of interest has to be ruled out before anything can be priced, which no other business here needs.

Photo & video studio

A call or a location visit, then a proposal.

Price depends on where the pictures end up rather than how long the shoot takes. The client who asks for “just a few photos” is usually the one planning a national campaign.

Those disagreements are the interesting part. A physiotherapist and an electrician run the same five steps on paper, but the moment where a person has to step in is not in the same place, and it is not there for the same reason.

The example messages are the good bit

They are not filler. Each one is built to test something specific, and each says so in the file. There is a message with no contact details that must be refused. One that is clearly outside the service area. One from a different canton that looks outside the area but should be accepted anyway.

And in every set, one emergency. In the physiotherapy set it is a description of symptoms that needs a person immediately, written calmly enough that a careless system would treat it as routine and offer an appointment. If the automation ever does that, it is worth writing about rather than quietly fixing.

04

What it measures

The quote-drafting workflow keeps a record of every draft it writes and every version a person approved, side by side.

So for each price that goes out, there is a record of what the computer suggested, what the human changed, and how long the check took. Over a few weeks that turns into a picture of exactly where the machine was trusted and where it was not.

If the corrections to one part of the price stop coming after a fortnight while corrections to another part carry on, that is the result. It shows where trust was earned, with numbers behind it rather than an opinion.

Worth knowing

The workflows have been checked carefully, but nobody has run them on a live system yet. The first real import will turn up a couple of small things to fix. That is normal, and what needed fixing is useful material for the write-up.
