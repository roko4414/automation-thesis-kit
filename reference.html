Five workflows, and the argument for where they stop

00

Get it running

Roughly an hour, most of it credentials. Nothing here needs a build step — the workflows are plain JSON files you import through the n8n editor.

  1. Check your n8n version first. Every typeVersion in these files is pinned to a value that exists in older releases too, but a version your instance does not know will import and then behave strangely rather than failing loudly. 
    n8n --version
  2. Make a Google Sheet with tabs named jobs, unqualified, events, rate_card, historical_jobs, quote_drafts, quote_reviews, scheduling, quotes_sent, followups, invoices. Paste the two CSVs into rate_card and historical_jobs.
  3. Replace the placeholder everywhere it appears — it is deliberately obvious: 
    grep -rl REPLACE_WITH_YOUR_SPREADSHEET_ID workflows/ \
  | xargs sed -i '' 's/REPLACE_WITH_YOUR_SPREADSHEET_ID/<your sheet id>/g'
  4. Validate before importing. This catches the failure modes that let an import "succeed" while quietly dropping half your wiring: 
    python3 scripts/validate-workflows.py workflows/*.json
  5. Import each file: Workflows → ⋯ → Import from File. Start with W1.
  6. Set credentials by hand. No credentials ship in these files, on purpose — see the reference. Open every Anthropic, Sheets and Email node and pick yours. Do not trust the auto-attach toast; n8n binds the most recently updated credential of the right type, which may not be the one you want.
  7. Test W1 with the sample enquiries. Three must behave specifically: ENQ-006 is refused, ENQ-011 escalates as a safety emergency, ENQ-008 is out of area. If ENQ-011 enters the normal quote flow, that is not a bug to quietly fix — write it down, it is a result.
Honest status

These files are validated structurally — envelope, node shapes, connection integrity, AI cluster wiring, typeVersion pins — but nobody has imported them into a live n8n instance yet. Import one end to end before building a week of work on top. Expect to fix something.

02

How the boundary was placed

Every gate above comes from the same three-axis score. This is the part that makes it a thesis rather than a build log — an examiner can disagree with a score, which is a far better position than disagreeing with your whole design.

Scoring axes, 1–5
AxisLow (1)High (5)
Variance
how much do instances differ?		Same every timeEvery one is a judgement call
Consequence
what does a wrong output cost?		Trivially correctedMoney, safety or reputation
Data availability
is the input structured and reachable?		In someone's headSitting in an API
  • Low variance, low consequence, high availability → fully automate.
  • High variance, low consequence → draft-and-review. The system proposes, the human disposes.
  • Any high consequence → human decides; automation only assembles the evidence.
  • Low data availability → do not automate yet. The prior project is capturing the data — and saying so in a thesis is a finding, not a failure.

In a high-variance, low-data-maturity small business, where should the boundary between automated and human decision sit — and what evidence justifies placing it there?

Score your own observed business on these axes. If it scores differently, move the gate and say why. A boundary that moved because of evidence is the strongest thing you can put in a results chapter, and it is what separates this from a description of features.

03

What to measure

The workflows already write the tables. Four weeks of baseline before you switch anything on, or the results chapter has nothing to compare against.

Primary outcome

End-to-end quote-to-cash elapsed time. One number, moved by the system as a whole, robust to noise in any single step.

The chart that answers the question

W3 writes quote_reviews on every approval: what was drafted, what was approved, which fields changed, and how long review took. Plot override rate by field, by week.

If the owner stops correcting material quantities by week three but never stops correcting labour hours, you have located the boundary empirically rather than argued for it. Nobody else in your cohort will have that chart.

Confounds to handle before you claim anything

  • Hawthorne. The owner knows they are measured and will chase quotes harder. Lean on system-timestamped lags over anything they can improve by trying.
  • Seasonality. Trades are weather-dependent. Report job mix in both windows.
  • Selection. If complex jobs stay manual, your sample is the easy half. Route everything through it or report the split.
  • Win rate is contaminated by market conditions and you cannot control for them at n≈25. Report it, attribute nothing.
Non-negotiable

Label every figure measured, estimated or assumed, in every table. A stopwatch reading is measured. "About twenty minutes" is estimated. A loaded rate from an award wage plus on-costs is assumed. Presenting an estimate as a measurement is the fastest way to lose an examiner, and it is entirely avoidable.

Failure modes, and what each one actually means

  • No business by week 3. Switch to observe-only and say so in methods. Do not keep waiting into week 6.
  • No baseline by week 4. Fatal to the results chapter. Stop building and fix it.
  • W3 drafts are poor. A finding, not a failure. Report where model judgement was insufficient and what data would have been needed.
  • The owner stops using it. The most informative outcome available. Interview immediately, while the reason is fresh — adoption failure in SME automation is under-reported precisely because students bury it.

Theory anchors

  • BPM lifecycle — Dumas et al., Fundamentals of Business Process Management.
  • RPA task-selection criteria — your three-axis scoring is a contribution against this baseline.
  • Levels of automation — Parasuraman & Sheridan. This is where your question actually lives.
  • Technology adoption in SMEs — for the discussion, on why the constraint is so rarely the technology.
